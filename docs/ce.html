Framing and Attitude-Formation

  • Frames matter to elicit specific consideration about policy, Chong and Druckman (2007), leading opinion by highlighting considerations, Slothuus and de Vreese (2010)

Moralized Communication

  • Moral Reframing (persuasion): Feinberg and Willer (2015)…
  • Politician’s daily bread: communicating position to favorable audience (social media, fundraising)
  • Moralization to mobilize, not to persuade, K. Simonsen and Bonikowski (2022)

Attitude Formation

  • question of attitude strength, Petty and Krosnick (1995)
  • moralized beliefs are great examples of strong attitudes
  • bottom-up: great for representation, top-down channeled to one’s fans: problem

Morality and Ideological Thinking

Growing Ideological

  • Question of dynamic constraint, Coppock and Green (2022), changing more attitudes than one
  • Politicians set ideological norms, Groenendyk, Kimbrough, and Pickup (2023), favorable audiences often follow

Horizontal Constraint

  • Belief systems organized by higher-order values, Vishwanath (2025), Peffley and Hurwitz (1985)
  • Value systems lead to alienation more often when values are moralized, Jung and Clifford (2025)
  • Moralized policy: more principled, more attached to groups

Conflict Extension

Main Worry

Product of moralized policy communication: Robust attitudes, comprehensive worldviews, all mapped into partisan identities, Layman and Carsey (2002), Baldassarri and Gelman (2008), seminal: Schattschneider (1960)

This could result in more sorting and greater ideological rifts between party camps, Layman, Carsey, and Horowitz (2006), Levendusky (2010), Bergman (2021),

Hypotheses

 

H1: Moralized policy communication strengthens attitude robustness and ideological congruence among favorable audiences on issues where prior attitudes are weak.

 

H2: Moralized policy communication increases attitudinal constraint across related issues among favorable audiences.

 

H3: Moralized policy communication shifts favorable audiences’ priorities toward policy goals and away from consensus-seeking and adherence to democratic norms.

Research Design

  1. Describe the prevalence and temporal trends in the use of moralized policy communication.
  2. Experimentally test the effects of moralized policy communication on the belief systems of predisposed (favorable) audiences.

Methods

  1. Computational analysis of elite communication
  2. Survey experimental design

Moralization in Policy Communication

Methods

  • E-newsletters, direct channel to constituents, unmoderated, widely used, favorable audiences (U.S. settings)
  • DCInbox dataset: coordinated effort to collect all these newsletters from 2010 until now (N = 179,767; after cleaning)
  • Splitting into segments: two sentence windows, tokenized, tf–idf and splitting in places of major semantic similarity drops (N = 754,809)
  • thorough cleaning (code, spanish, old encodings, etc.)

Data

Policy Classifier

  • Comparative Agendas Project categories
  • Transformer-based classifier by Aroyehun et al. (2025)
  • smaller categories grouped into larger ones based on thematic proximity (e.g. agriculture, 20 to 8 overall)
  • preliminary validation looks positive

Moralization and Conflict Extension

Experimental Design

  • For a limited time, repeatedly send excerpts from real newsletters on a specific topic, then survey them (N = 2,500)
  • Some will be surveyed repeatedly on different issues
  • Newsletters will always come from co-partisans
  • Respondents will be randomized to receive either moralized or non-moralized communication
  • Randomization to policy issue from a list, restricted to issues where they lack crystallized opinions

Example Experimental Stimulus: Housing

New York City is the epicenter of America’s housing crisis, with nearly half of New Yorkers spending a third of their income or more on rent. Even more unacceptable is the fact that nearly 25 percent of New Yorkers spend more than half of their income on rent. These astronomically high housing costs are placing a huge financial burden on New York’s families. And while America’s Public Housing Authorities are far from perfect, with plenty of issues of their own that government must address, exorbitantly high costs make public housing all the more important. […] Every family in New York deserves to live with respect and dignity in their homes, and delivering real results for these families must be a priority for this Congress.

Goldman, Dan, Democrat (NY), 24. 8. 2023

Surveys

Attitude Robustness

Guttman scales. Scenario of growing extremity.

  1. When a tenant falls behind on rent due to unexpected expenses, landlords should have to provide a grace period and clear notice before eviction. [GRACE PERIOD]

  2. Landlords should be allowed to evict tenants only for just cause (e.g., nonpayment or lease violations), not simply to raise the rent for a new renter. [JUST CAUSE]

  3. When rents rise sharply, the government should require landlords to provide advance notice and limit unusually large rent increases. [ADVANCE NOTICE]

  4. The government should strictly regulate rents, setting clear caps on how much landlords can charge and increase each year. [FULL REGULATION]

Surveys

Dynamic Constraint

  1. Employers should treat workers fairly and avoid sudden or arbitrary terminations that leave employees without time to adjust. [EMPLOYMENT]

  2. Health insurance companies should not impose sudden or excessive cost burdens on patients, especially when people depend on stable coverage. [HEALTHCARE]

  3. The government should crack down on oil and fuel companies that sharply raise gas prices during emergencies or supply shortages. [ENERGY]

Surveys

Stakes in Policy

  • Considering several options
  • Most likely a conjoint-style design to assess trade-offs between (moralized) policy positions and:
    1. adherence to democratic norms
    2. willingness to seek bipartisan consensus
  • Other options include incorporating a game-based task or using simple attitudinal scales.
